# Alternative: the sleeper continues

**Status: adopted as version 3** (`script-v3.md`, `script-v3.html`).
`script-v2.md` and `script-v2.html` keep the erasure reading. This file
records how version 3 was derived and the alternatives that were rejected;
`script-v3.md` is the reference for the adopted text. One difference from
the plan below: the FAQ became two slides, 28 and 28b, because ten
questions didn't fit on one card.

## Why consider it

Under the erasure reading, an observer who lives through H3 sees Zelda at
the temple gate in scene 18 with no H3 history behind her. Her only route
from the desert escape (10) to that departure was the crystal, and rule 5
discontinues that stage at Demise's defeat. The departure survives only
because rule 2 lets a protected crossing keep a cause from superseded
history: her awakening, which H3 no longer contains.

This variant closes that gap. The crystal-bound Zelda lives through H3
instead of being erased.

## The change

Demise's defeat in the distant past does not end Zelda's sleep, because it
does not end Demise entirely. Her sleep sustains the seal on Demise, and
after the final battle his residual consciousness is sealed in the Master
Sword. For thousands of years the sword, with Fi asleep in it, and Zelda's
sleep hold that remnant together.

Link's wish ends it. The wish stays effective at its original time
(rule 3), and it destroys whatever remains of Demise at that moment. In H2
that is the Imprisoned. In H3 it is the remnant in the sword. With nothing
of Demise left to seal, Zelda's duty as keeper of the seal is fulfilled,
and she wakes.

In H3 she therefore sleeps through the revised centuries, wakes at the
original moment of the wish as the statue lands on the empty Sealed
Grounds, and is the Zelda Ghirahim seizes in 18. Her earlier stage reaches
her own crossing, the same pattern as Impa and the sword, so nobody has
to be erased.

## Rules

Four rules instead of five.

1. **History can be replaced.** Unchanged.
2. **Completed time journeys survive revision.** Unchanged, except that
   the closing sentences pointing to rule 5 are dropped. The rule ends at:
   “This is a proposed continuity rule, not a guarantee of safety from
   ordinary harm.”
3. **A fulfilled Triforce wish survives at its original time.** Refined:
   the wish stays effective at its original time, and what it acts on
   follows the revised history (rule 4). The descended statue and the
   assembled Triforce are its physical result in both histories. What it
   destroys is Demise in whatever form he exists at that moment.
4. **Other consequences follow the revised history.** Unchanged. Zelda
   waking once nothing of Demise remains is one of these consequences.

Rule 5 is dropped. It existed only for the crystal, and the closing card
already admits it “was written for the crystal.”

Rule 5 is replaced by a reading of the wish and the seal rather than a
rule about time:

> **Link's wish destroys whatever remains of Demise at the moment it takes
> effect, and Zelda's sleep lasts until nothing of him remains to seal.**

Two limits keep this consistent with the game:

- **The wish does not reach backward.** Ghirahim still revives Demise in
  the distant past, which is earlier in calendar time than the wish.
- **The wish does not end the curse.** Demise's curse is a promise that
  his hatred will return, not a part of him existing at the moment of the
  wish. The later games are untouched.

What comes from the game:

- The Triforce grants the wish of whoever holds it, and Hylia's plan was
  for it to be used against Demise.
- Zelda sleeps to keep Demise sealed, and wakes right after the wish.
- After the final battle, Demise's residual consciousness is sealed in
  the Master Sword.

What the theory adds:

- The wish survives revision at its original time (rule 3). The game
  never shows a wish crossing time.
- The wish reaches the remnant in the sword. In the game, Fi describes
  that remnant fading slowly in the blade; in H3 the wish completes the
  fading at its original time. Fi's long sleep continues; the game never
  says what happens after.
- The seal Zelda sustains follows Demise into the sword. The game never
  says what her seal holds after the final battle.

To an observer who lives through H3, the statue falling with no one seen
making the wish is strange. Within the theory it is not a gap: it is the
wish's preserved result arriving at its original time, as rule 3 states.

This is not the version 1 idea of a crystal immune to rewrites. She is not
protected from revision. She simply lives through H3, as Impa does.

### A path considered and rejected: the leak

Her sleep could also be justified by restoring the version 1 leak: Demise's
residue seeps out of the Master Sword and re-forms the Imprisoned, so H3's
present matches H2's. Link would then make the wish himself, and rule 3
and W1 would no longer be needed. It was rejected because it contradicts
Fi's account of the sword sealing the residue, needs a reason for the leak
to stop after the wish, and turns the finale back into an ordinary closed
loop.

## What the observer sees in H3

**Already history when the tornado hits:**

- At the final battle, two Zeldas are in the distant past: one asleep in
  the crystal, one rescued from Ghirahim's ritual. Link defeats Demise,
  and what remains of him is sealed in the Master Sword. The crystal
  stays closed. The rescued Zelda gives Impa the bracelet and leaves with
  Link and Groose.
- Since then, Impa has guarded the crystal and a visible pedestal sword
  for thousands of years. The Life Tree has grown in the temple, Lanayru
  never died, and there is no Imprisoned.

**From the tornado on:**

- The tornado, young Impa's protection, and the springs: unchanged, still
  the simplest reading rather than fixed by the rules.
- **09:** Link finds Old Impa, the pedestal sword, the grown tree, and the
  crystal with Zelda inside.
- **10, 11/12, 14/15:** unchanged protected crossings.
- Link's search for the Triforce now has an H3 reason: Zelda asked him to
  find it (05), and she is still asleep, waiting.
- **W1:** at the original moment of the wish, the statue lands on empty
  grounds with the Triforce. The remnant in the sword fades, and Zelda
  wakes from the crystal.
- **18:** Ghirahim seizes the newly awakened Zelda and takes her into the
  past. Link and Groose follow. Every motive in this scene exists in H3.
- **25–26:** unchanged. The crystal is empty because she woke at W1, and
  the sword in the pedestal no longer holds anything of Demise.

Zelda's line in H3 is unbroken: Skyloft → tornado → desert gate (10) →
distant past (02–05) → crystal → wakes (W1) → seized (18) → distant past
(19–23) → home (25).

## What it fixes

- **Zelda's departure in 18 has an H3 cause.** The biggest gap in the
  erasure reading disappears.
- **The trio's crossing in 18 needs no inherited motive.** Ghirahim takes
  Zelda, and Link and Groose follow, as in the game.
- **Her sleep has a purpose and a natural end.** It seals the remnant the
  game places in the Master Sword, and it ends when that remnant is gone,
  because her duty is done.
- **One wish, two histories.** The same wish destroys Demise in whatever
  form he exists at its moment: the Imprisoned in H2, the remnant in H3.
- **Zelda's memories match the world she returns to.** She slept through
  every Imprisoned attack in H2, so almost nothing she remembers is
  missing from H3. Only the moment she wakes differs: the statue lands on
  the Imprisoned in H2 and on empty ground in H3.
- **The theory needs one rule fewer,** and loses the rule that was written
  for a single case.

## What it costs

- **The theory loses its most distinctive claim.** Erasure was its answer
  to the sleeping Zelda who is never seen again. In this variant she is
  still there after the final battle, unseen, which is the conventional
  reading. She stops being something the theory explains and becomes an
  ordinary part of the loop. Scene 22, “the theory's boldest step,” goes
  away.
- **Her seal overlaps with the sword's.** For thousands of years she is a
  second lock on a remnant the sword already holds.
- **The wish's reach is the theory's reading.** That it acts across a
  revision, and reaches the remnant in the sword, comes from rule 3, not
  from the game. Its two limits (not backward, not the curse) have to be
  stated, or it would undo the past revival and the later games.
- **W1 does more work.** The preserved wish now ends the remnant and wakes
  Zelda as well as bringing the statue down.

**Gaps this variant does not fix:**

- The seedling and Lanayru's cure still rely on causes inherited from H0
  and H1.
- Old Impa's silence still rests on her choice, as in the FAQ entry
  “Why doesn't Old Impa warn anyone about what's coming?” Here she also
  knows that Ghirahim will take Zelda moments after the crystal opens.
- Ghirahim's possible knowledge of his own failure is still unexplained.

### Scenes

Proposed text for the scenes that change. Scenes not listed stay as they
are.

**00** — In the opening card and narration, replace “the sleeping Zelda
who is never seen again” with “the sleeping Zelda left behind after the
final battle.”

**20** — Staging: the sleeping Zelda remains present through and after
the correction.

**22 — Two Zeldas, one life**

*Caption:* Demise is defeated, but what is left of him now lies sealed in
the sword, and the crystal stays closed. The Zelda asleep inside is the
one Link just rescued, at an earlier point in her life. She will hold the
seal until nothing of Demise remains, and only then live the days that
brought her here.

*Staging:* No dissolve. Show the sleeper in the crystal and the rescued
Zelda with the trio. A faint link joins the crystal to the pedestal sword,
and a line connects the sleeper forward to W1 and 18.

*Narration:* Demise is defeated, but what is left of him is sealed in the
sword, and the crystal stays closed. The Zelda asleep inside is the one
Link just rescued, at an earlier point in her life. She will hold the seal
until nothing of Demise remains, and only then live the days that brought
her here.

**23** — Staging: the crystal remains in its sealed room, offstage. The
ending footage doesn't show it, which fits this variant.

**24 — The revised centuries**

*Caption:* Impa grows old guarding the sword and the sleeping Zelda, who
together hold what is left of Demise. In this revised history, the
present-day Imprisoned attacks do not occur. She remembers the ancient
conflict and the friends whose return she is waiting to see.

*Staging:* The crystal stays visible in the temple beside the sword and
the tree.

*Narration:* Impa grows old a second time, but in a different history: one
without the Imprisoned's attacks, where the Master Sword was always there.
She guards it and the sleeping Zelda, who hold what is left of Demise
between them, and waits for the friends she knows will come back.

**W1 — The wish outlives its history**

*Caption:* The corrected history reaches the moment of Link's wish. The
Goddess Statue descends onto empty grounds, and the wish finds what is
left of Demise in the sword and ends it. With nothing left to seal, Zelda
wakes from her crystal.

*Staging:* The statue lands; the remnant leaves the pedestal sword and
fades in the Triforce's light; the link between crystal and sword fades;
the crystal opens. Optionally follow with a labeled reprise of 18 to show
the seizure now happens in H3 too.

*Narration:* When this corrected history reaches the moment of Link's
wish, the statue still comes down, only now onto empty ground. The wish
still does what Link asked: what is left of Demise, sealed in the sword,
finally fades. With nothing left to seal, Zelda wakes from her crystal.

**25** — Caption: replace “guarding the sword, not fighting those
present-day attacks” with “guarding the sword and Zelda's sleep, not
fighting those present-day attacks.” Narration: “Old Impa, in this
theory, remembers a different vigil: guarding a sword and a sleeping
girl, not fighting a beast.”

**26** — Drop the contrast with the crystal. Caption: “Impa recognizes
Zelda and reveals the bracelet. Her long duty is complete, and she fades
away. The Master Sword remains.” Narration: “Impa recognizes Zelda, and
shows her the bracelet. Her duty is done, and she fades away, leaving it
behind.”

**27 — closing card**

- **Four rules:** history can be replaced; completed time journeys
  survive; the wish survives at its original time; other consequences
  follow the revised history.
- **Limits:**
  - The earlier farewell and failure are hypothetical.
  - The traveler and wish rules are assumptions.
  - That the wish reaches Demise's remnant in the sword, and that Zelda's
    seal holds it until then, are the theory's readings.
  - The corrected present between the preserved crossings is left open.

*Narration:* So that is the theory. History can be rewritten, but journeys
through time survive, and a fulfilled wish keeps its result. None of it is
confirmed by the game. It's simply one possible way to make the pieces fit
without breaking established lore or getting too deep into speculation.

**28 — FAQ**

- Replace “Why doesn't the sleeping Zelda just wake up early?” with:
  **Why doesn't the sleeping Zelda wake as soon as Demise is defeated?**
  Because Demise isn't entirely gone: what is left of him is sealed in the
  Master Sword, and she keeps holding it. At the original time of the wish,
  the wish ends that remnant too (rule 3). With nothing left to seal, she
  wakes, and becomes the Zelda Ghirahim takes into the past.
- Replace the answer to “why do the three still travel to the past?”
  with: Ghirahim still seizes the newly woken Zelda and takes her into the
  past, and Link and Groose follow. The rest of the corrected present
  between the crossings is left open.
- Extend the answer to “Does anyone make the wish again?”: No. The statue
  descends once, at the original moment, as the surviving result of
  Link's wish (rule 3). In the corrected history, the same wish ends what
  is left of Demise in the sword.
- Extend the answer to “Does this change Demise's curse or the later
  games?”: No. The wish ends what remains of Demise at its moment, but the
  curse is a promise that his hatred will return, not a part of him. The
  curse, the Master Sword and the Triforce carry on as the game ends.
