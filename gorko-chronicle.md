# Field notes of Gorko, surface explorer

*The corrected history (H3) under the current, erasure version of the
theory, seen from the ground. Gorko knows only what he sees, what he is
told, and the legends he has studied. Where he can't account for
something, the theory has a gap. For the version where the sleeping Zelda
continues, see `gorko-chronicle-alternative.md`.*

---

**On what I knew before the storm**

For anyone who reads these notes later, here is what every Goron of the surface already knew before any of this began.

The Sealed Grounds are a quiet place. There is a great spiral pit there, and the old tales say something terrible was once sealed beneath it. It was destroyed long ago, in the age of the goddess, by a hero no one can name. Nothing stirs in that pit now but wind.

Beside the pit stands the Sealed Temple. An old woman of the Sheikah lives there, and as far as I know she has always lived there. My grandfather's grandfather spoke of her. She guards a sword set in a stone pedestal and lets no one touch it. A great tree grows right inside the temple, its roots through the floor and its crown up to the roof. I've measured its trunk. Nothing that size grows in a lifetime.

The Thunder Dragon lives up in the storm clouds over Lanayru. He has done so for as long as there have been stories about him.

One more thing, the strangest. Some days before the storm, I was camping at the edge of the Sealed Grounds. I saw a light inside the temple, and a young Sheikah woman came out. She had the same eye on her clothes, the same bearing, even the same way of standing as the old woman. She set off toward Faron Woods without a word. I asked the old woman who she was, and she only smiled.

**The storm**

Today a black whirlwind tore open the clouds above Faron. I have never seen weather like it. It didn't drift, it *hunted*. Something fell out of it: a girl, a speck of pink, dropping through the sky into the forest. The whirlwind closed up and vanished as if it had finished a job.

I searched until dark and found nothing. I hope the forest was kind to her.

**The boy in green**

A boy came down from the sky today on a red bird, dressed all in green, with a blue blade on his back that spoke to him. I heard it speak. He asked everyone about a girl named Zelda. That must be the girl from the storm.

He went to the old woman first. Whatever she told him, he came out looking like someone who'd been handed a map with half of it torn off. I told him what I know about the goddess and her inscriptions. He listened politely, the way young people do.

**The white-haired man**

There is a man in the woods. He has white hair and dresses like a nobleman going to a dance, but he fights like a demon. The boy crossed blades with him near the forest temple. I heard the man call himself a Demon Lord, which seemed a bit much. He is after the girl, I'm sure of it. I think that storm was his doing too. A wind that hunts is not a natural wind.

The forest dwellers tell me the girl was there. She stopped at the spring with the young Sheikah woman and left before the boy arrived. They say she wore white now, not pink, and looked like someone who had remembered something enormous.

**The desert**

I was in Lanayru when it happened, so I can testify to this one myself.

The girl and the Sheikah woman ran to the old Temple of Time with the Demon Lord close behind. The boy arrived just in time and held him off. Behind them a gate of light opened. The two women stepped into it, and then the gate *shattered*. They destroyed it themselves on the way through.

They didn't run away somewhere. They vanished from the world. I examined the pieces of the gate afterward. That light went somewhere far beyond the desert. I'd say it went into some other age.

The Demon Lord left in a rage. The boy left with a harp the young woman had given him.

**The flames, and a sword I recognized**

The boy has visited the three sacred flames, one in each province. Each time he came back, his blade was brighter. After the last one it had a new shape.

I've stared at that shape for most of my life. It's the sword in the old woman's pedestal: same guard, same blade. Only his is new and shining, and hers is old and still. Two of the same sword, one in a boy's hand and one in a stone. I can't account for it, and the old woman wouldn't let me ask.

**The temple gate**

With the new sword the boy woke a gate inside the Sealed Temple, the same light the young Sheikah woman came out of. He stepped in and was gone.

Not long after, he stepped back out. He looked older around the eyes, as if days had passed for him that never passed for us.

He has done this more than once. Once he came back carrying a tiny seedling, went in, and returned with empty hands. The old woman looked up at her great tree when he did that, and I swear she was smiling.

**What the boy was doing after that, I can't say**

He spent a long time in places a Goron can't follow, up in the sky and in trials the old woman called "silent." I heard he went to the gorge where the Thunder Dragon once lived, though what he hoped to find there I don't know. The dragon lives in the clouds and always has.

What he was searching for, and why, he never told me. I don't think anyone here could have told me either.

**The goddess comes down**

Today the sky fell.

I was at the Sealed Grounds and heard it before I saw it, a roar like a mountain clearing its throat. Then the statue of the goddess, the great statue the sky people's island is built around, came down through the clouds and landed in the spiral pit. The ground shook all the way to Eldin. A golden light blazed above it, three triangles.

It landed on *nothing*. There was nothing in that pit. The legends say the darkness down there was destroyed ages ago, and I've walked every step of that spiral myself. Whatever the goddess meant to crush, she was crushing an empty hole.

I looked all around for whoever had called her down. I saw no one. If somebody did this, they did it out of my sight.

**The girl who couldn't be there**

I have to write this exactly as I saw it, because I don't believe it myself.

Shortly after the statue fell, I was at the Sealed Temple. The Demon Lord appeared at the gate, and he had the girl. Zelda, the same girl from the desert, in white, who had walked into a gate of light and shattered it behind her. Nobody saw her come back. The old woman's door was shut the whole time. There was no other road, and yet there she was.

He dragged her through the temple gate. The boy went after them, and so did a tall Skyloft lad with hair like a breaking wave, who had shown up on the surface somewhere along the way. All four were gone in a moment.

The old woman didn't move. She didn't cry out or reach for the girl. She watched the gate like someone watching a play she has seen a thousand times.

**The return**

The gate stayed lit. I stayed too.

Three people came back out: the boy, the girl, and the tall lad. The boy's hands were empty, and his sword wasn't with him. The girl looked at the great tree, at the pedestal, and at the old woman, like someone coming home to a house where the furniture has been moved.

The old woman stood up. She called the girl by name, as tenderly as I've ever heard a name spoken. She showed her a bracelet on her wrist, and the girl knew it at once. They spoke in a low voice. I didn't come closer, and I won't write down what I didn't hear.

Then the old woman began to fade, like morning fog. She was smiling. Then she was gone, and the bracelet stayed where she had stood.

The sword is still in its pedestal. The gate has gone dark and I don't think it will light again.

**What I can't answer**

I'm an explorer, not a seer, so let me be honest about what I don't know.

I don't know how the girl came back to be taken a second time. Every path I know of was closed to her.

I don't know who asked the goddess to come down, or why she came down on an empty pit.

And I don't know why the old woman said nothing. She knew. I'm certain of it, from the way she watched that gate. She had all the time in the world to warn the boy, and she kept it all to herself. I think she knew something we didn't: that some things, if you stop them, you lose everything that came after them.

When the three of them talk, they speak of a great beast in the pit and of a girl asleep in crystal. I've lived beside that pit all my life, and I never saw either one. But I believe them. They talk like people who remember a world that is no longer here.

*— Gorko*
