# Skyward Sword: the self-correcting timeline theory

**Version 3, the sleeper continues — script and presentation.**

Version 3 keeps version 2's revisable history, protected journeys, earlier
attempts, and preserved wish, and changes one thing: the crystal-bound
Zelda is no longer erased. She sleeps through the corrected history,
holding the seal on what is left of Demise in the Master Sword, and wakes
when Link's wish, preserved at its original time, ends that remnant. Rule
5 is no longer needed, so the theory has four rules.

`script-v3.html` implements this document. `script-v2.md` and
`script-v2.html` remain as the erasure version; `alternative-sleeper-continues.md`
records how this version was derived, and the alternatives that were rejected.

## Premise

One timeline, capable of revision. Journeys into the past can fulfill
existing history or replace it. The travelers can remember an adventure
that the corrected world no longer remembers.

“Self-correcting” is the name of this interpretation, not a confirmed law
stated by the game. This is not a strictly immutable closed loop. It is a
single revisable history containing causal loops, with no surviving branches.
The starting history inherits an earlier attempt that the game does not
show: this same journey as history first recorded it, which ended in a
resealing. The adventure we follow changes that journey, completes the
Triforce wish and Demise's defeat, and leaves a history whose ending is the
one its own travelers reach, so nothing is left to correct.

**Opening caption:**

> What if time preserves the travelers, but rewrites the world they return to?

**Persistent small label:** `A proposed reading of the game's time travel`

## The four rules

1. **History can be replaced.** There is one currently realized history.
   Superseded versions do not remain as parallel worlds, although they
   can be the origin of surviving travelers and effects.
2. **Completed time journeys survive revision.** A traveler retains their
   identity, experienced history, carried objects, and departure and
   arrival events. Revised personal histories connect to those crossings
   rather than generating additional continuing counterparts. This is a
   proposed continuity rule, not a guarantee of safety from ordinary harm.
3. **A fulfilled Triforce wish survives at its original time.** Link's wish
   stays effective when it originally took effect, even if its original
   cause belongs to a superseded history. What it acts on follows the
   revised history (rule 4). The descended statue and assembled Triforce
   are its physical result in every history; what it destroys is Demise,
   in whatever form he exists at that moment.
4. **Other consequences follow the revised history.** A planted seedling
   grows, a cured dragon survives the illness, deposited objects age, and
   residents live through the resulting events. Neither a completed mission
   nor a previous encounter with time travel makes everything immune.

Two ages of one person may coexist, and no rule removes anyone for being
present. Version 2's fifth rule, “one traveler, one continuing line,”
existed only to erase the crystal-bound Zelda; this version doesn't need it.

### The wish and the seal

> **Link's wish destroys whatever remains of Demise at the moment it takes
> effect, and Zelda's sleep lasts until nothing of him remains to seal.**

- In H2, what remains of Demise at the moment of the wish is the
  Imprisoned. The statue destroys it, and Zelda wakes.
- In H3, Demise was defeated in the distant past, and what remains is his
  residual consciousness sealed in the Master Sword. The same wish ends
  it at the same moment, and Zelda wakes.

Two limits keep this consistent with the game:

- **The wish does not reach backward.** Ghirahim still revives Demise in
  the distant past, which is earlier in calendar time than the wish.
- **The wish does not end the curse.** Demise's curse is a promise that
  his hatred will return, not a part of him existing at the moment of the
  wish. The later games are untouched.

What comes from the game: the Triforce grants its holder's wish, and
Hylia's plan was for it to be used against Demise; Zelda sleeps to keep
Demise sealed and wakes right after the wish; after the final battle,
Demise's residual consciousness is sealed in the Master Sword.

What the theory adds: the wish survives revision at its original time
(rule 3); it reaches the remnant in the sword, completing the slow fading
Fi describes; and the seal Zelda sustains follows Demise into the sword.
The game never shows a wish crossing time, and never says what her seal
holds after the final battle.

For thousands of years, then, Zelda's seal overlaps with the sword's: she
is a second lock on a remnant the sword already holds. Say so rather than
hiding it.

To an observer who lives through H3, the statue falling with no one seen
making the wish is strange. Within the theory it is not a gap: it is the
wish's preserved result arriving at its original time.

## Agreed interpretation

0. The history the adventure starts in (H0) was shaped by an earlier
   attempt: this same journey, made by the same Zelda, Link, Impa and
   Groose, as history first recorded it. That version matched ours up to
   Link's first visit, but nobody planted the seedling or cured the
   dragon, and its final battle only resealed Demise. Old Impa's early
   bracelet comes from that version's farewell. Why it ended in a
   resealing is unknown. See “The earlier attempt” below.
1. Hylia entrusts Impa and Fi with her plan. In this reconstruction, Impa
   waits to meet Hylia's future incarnation before beginning her own journey.
2. Zelda and the returning Impa reach the past. The returning Impa meets
   her younger self; the younger Impa leaves for the present, while the
   returning Impa stays with Zelda. These are stages of one person's life.
3. Zelda's crystal vigil sustains the history in which the Imprisoned
   survives until Link's era.
4. Link's planting and Lanayru's cure change history. The tree was not
   hidden, and the dragon's bones genuinely belonged to the earlier history.
5. The finale occurs shortly after the first temple visit in calendar time,
   even though Link has experienced much more of his adventure between visits.
6. Demise's defeat and containment in the Master Sword prevent his subsequent
   existence as the present-day Imprisoned. There is no leak from the blade.
7. At this correction, the crystal-bound Zelda is not erased. She sleeps
   on through H3, holding the seal on the remnant in the Master Sword, and
   wakes at the original moment of the wish, when the wish ends that
   remnant. She is the Zelda Ghirahim seizes in 18.
8. Link, Groose, and the rescued Zelda retain their identities, bodies,
   carried objects, memories, and completed time crossings through the
   correction. Their return continues those journeys, without a second
   native trio waiting alongside them.
9. Impa lives through the revised centuries, guarding the sword and the
   sleeping Zelda. She
   remembers the ancient conflict and the trio's departure, but, under
   this interpretation, not the erased present-day Imprisoned attacks.
10. Old Impa's final disappearance ends her fulfilled vigil. It is not
    evidence that she has been erased from all history.
11. The corrected world retains the wish's result at its original time.
    An observer sees the statue descend onto empty grounds, the remnant
    in the sword fade, and Zelda wake, followed later by the trio's
    return. No second wish is needed in the corrected history.
12. Hylia's Goddess Sword is strengthened during Link's adventure. The
    theory does not require a Master Sword to lose its upgrades, reset Fi,
    or return to Hylia's statue between attempts. Only the final intervention
    necessarily includes the sword's deposition shown in the ending.

## Rules and limits that must remain explicit

### Revision is ordered by the adventure, not just the calendar

The presentation follows three acts and the order in which interventions
are made. It must not claim to show one immutable chronology from an
outside observer's perspective, as version 1 did.

Use these history labels where needed:

- **H0 — before the planting:** the temple has no grown Life Tree; Lanayru's
  bones are in the gorge.
- **H1 — after the planting:** the tree has grown at the temple; Lanayru
  has not yet been cured.
- **H2 — after the cure:** Lanayru survived the illness. This is the
  history from which the final kidnapping and pursuit depart.
- **H3 — after Demise's defeat:** the later Imprisoned attacks are removed
  by the proposed correction. Zelda's crystal vigil continues, holding the
  remnant in the sword, until the wish ends it.

These are successive versions of one history, not coexisting universes.
Use **P — earlier attempts** for the version of the journey that H0
inherits: the same journey as history first recorded it, ending in a
resealing. P is not another branch, and its people are not other travelers.
Whether P itself replaced an even earlier version is unknown. H0–H3 track
revisions within the adventure being presented; they are not four
additional full resealing attempts.

An earlier version is no longer an independently existing world after
replacement. References to it identify the origin of surviving memories,
arrivals, and effects, rather than another universe that remains accessible.

### Protection is more than being physically in the past

Both Zeldas are in the past at the final intervention, and both continue.
The rescued Zelda is the later stage: she retains her experiences and
returns home through a protected crossing (rule 2). The sleeping Zelda is
the earlier stage: she lives through H3 (rule 4), wakes at the wish, and
reaches her own departure in 18. One person, two ages, one unbroken line,
the same pattern as young and old Impa.

Rule 2 protects completed departures as well as arrivals. It is possible
for a protected crossing to retain a cause from superseded history rather
than acquire a new motive in H3. Do not invent a second Imprisoned attack
to force the trio to leave again in the corrected account.

This distinction is a proposed rule, not an established game mechanic.
Avoid the general statement “time deletes people who no longer have a
purpose.” Their causal relationship to the rewritten history matters.

Impa is not permanently immune simply because she once used a gate. She
can remember her completed journey and subsequently live through a revised
future. Ghirahim's arrival must likewise survive long enough to cause the
ritual; protection from rewriting is not protection from defeat.

### Deposited objects become part of the resulting history

The seedling grows after Link plants it. The sword ages after Link leaves
it. Zelda's bracelet stays with Impa. None is automatically deleted when
it stops being carried by a traveler.

The same holds for the sleeping Zelda: nothing deletes what stays behind
in the past. Do not suggest a blanket “anything left in the past vanishes”
rule.

### The earlier attempt: the journey as history first recorded it

This is the part of the theory most specific to it, and the presentation
explains it in full after the story ends (L1–L3), with a teaser at P0.

**Why there must be an earlier attempt.** At the start of the adventure
(H0), Old Impa already wears the bracelet Zelda only gives her at the very
end. If H0's past already contained the ending the audience watches, Demise
would have been destroyed in the distant past and the Imprisoned couldn't
be waiting under the Sealed Grounds. It is there, together with Lanayru's
bones and no Life Tree. So the farewell Old Impa remembers belongs to a
different ending: one with the same bracelet, a final battle that only
resealed Demise, and no planting or cure before it.

**Who made it.** The same people. The earlier attempt is this very journey
as history first recorded it: the same Zelda fled through the desert gate,
the same Link met her on his first visit, the same Impa kept watch, and
the same trio went into the past at the end. Everything up to Link's
first visit (02–05) matches; H0 labels those scenes for that reason. The
consequences are:

- It is the same bracelet. No second, identical bracelet is needed.
- Nobody remembers an earlier attempt, because no separate travelers ever
  made one. Link changed the journey before he lived its ending, so no
  traveler carries that version. Only its traces stayed in the past: Old
  Impa's bracelet and memories, and the Imprisoned.
- In that version Link didn't leave his sword in the temple, which is why
  no aged blade waits there when the adventure begins.

**Why this attempt is the last.** That version held only as long as the
journey matched it. The first thing Link did differently, planting the
seedling, revised the past (H0 → H1); the cure revised it again (H1 →
H2); his final victory replaced the resealing (H2 → H3). In H3 the ending
the past remembers is the one the journey actually reaches, so there is
nothing left to correct. This is the “self-correcting timeline” in its
plainest form: history keeps revising until the ending it holds is the
ending its own travelers reach.

**What stays open.** Why the earlier final battle only resealed Demise,
and whether that version itself replaced an even earlier one, are unknown.
Do not invent the earlier battle's details, stage its farewell as a lost
scene from the game, or give Impa memories of several failed histories.

The handoff shown in the finale belongs to H3 and supplies the bracelet
for the revised Impa who greets the returning trio. The earlier farewell
and the H3 farewell are corresponding events in different versions of
history: do not stack both into one ancient day or give one Impa two
bracelets. Keep the bracelet on early Old Impa.

### The Triforce: the result and its time survive

**Proposed rule:** A fulfilled Triforce wish preserves its accomplished
result at the time it originally took effect, even if the history leading
to that wish is rewritten.

In H2, Link makes the wish and the statue descends upon the Imprisoned.
In H3, at the corresponding present-day moment, the statue descends onto
grounds where the beast is absent. The assembled Triforce accompanies it.
The result remains, although its cause belongs to the superseded history.

What the wish destroys follows the revised history. In H3 there is no
Imprisoned, but Demise's residual consciousness is still sealed in the
Master Sword, so the wish ends that instead, and Zelda's seal is released
with it. See “The wish and the seal” for the two limits: the wish does not
reach backward, and it does not end the curse.

Do not require another Link to make another wish in H3. Do not replay the
kneeling Link as a newly occurring native action unless a later revision
explicitly explains his personal history. The correction preserves the
result of the H2 wish, not automatically every step of the original quest.

The statue stays in the sky during the intervening centuries; it does
not descend immediately after the ancient duel. Only the specified wish
outcome and its timing receive this protection. Do not silently extend
it to every unrelated event, memory, or object in the game.

An observer who lives through H3 sees the ancient victory, the farewell,
the centuries of Impa's vigil over the sword and the crystal, the statue's
apparently unprompted descent, the remnant fading and Zelda waking, her
kidnapping, and then the trio's return. The observer need not remember H2.
An observer outside all revisions could compare H2 with H3, but that is
the presentation's viewpoint, not a new in-world character.

### Why the final attempt differs

The earlier version and the witnessed one are the same journey with a
different course, not a deterministic sequence that repeats until it
somehow improves. The earlier version ended in a resealing. The witnessed
adventure changes the past before the final battle, completes the Triforce
wish, and defeats Demise, sealing his remaining consciousness in the sword.

Why the earlier version only resealed Demise is unspecified. Do not claim
to know whether a particular trial failed or precisely when its hero
stopped, and don't assert that no wish of any kind was made in it. The
relevant distinction is that the earlier history did not contain this
completed resolution.

Do not give Impa memories of multiple failed histories, or claim that
repetition automatically makes Link stronger. Neither is needed here.
Do not say the wish erases Demise from every era: Ghirahim still revives
him in the ancient past before Link's final battle.

The earlier version did not include the planting or Lanayru's cure; that
is part of how the theory knows it differed (H0 has no tree and the bones
are still there).

### The sword: a revised history, not a physically reset blade

Hylia's Goddess Sword remains the blade's origin. Link strengthens it
during the witnessed adventure. Replacing later history does not require
the Master Sword to reverse its forging, return to the statue, or restore
Fi to an earlier mental state.

The earlier version's resealing does not entail the final pedestal scene.
In that version Link kept the blade, which is why no aged Master Sword
waits in the temple at the start of the adventure. Its full route is not
established. Do not draw a detailed earlier sword path as though it were
supported by the game.

The final deposition is the one shown: Link leaves his sword with Impa,
and it ages into the blade seen at the reunion. An older Master Sword
and a younger Goddess Sword can coexist as stages of one object. Replaced
histories do not by themselves deposit additional physical blades into H3.

Remove the power-loss/reforging cycle entirely. The earlier recurrence
concerns resealing Demise, not repeatedly resetting the same sword.

### The trio: departures and arrivals belong to the same protected journey

The corrected personal histories connect to the protected departures.
The returning Link, Zelda, and Groose continue those same identities;
they do not arrive beside three adults who independently stayed home.
This is the matching condition imposed by rule 2, not a new disappearance
or merger occurring when the trio steps out of the gate.

Their crossing events and memories may retain causes from H2, even where
the corresponding H3 circumstances differ. In this version the departure
in 18 doesn't need that allowance: Zelda wakes at W1, Ghirahim seizes her,
and Link and Groose follow, all within H3. The script does not invent
replacement motives or replay their entire childhoods to establish them.
This rule defines the proposed reconciliation; it is not shown being
explained by a character in the game.

Temporary coexistence remains allowed. Young and old Impa can meet, and
the two stages of Zelda coexist from the final battle until the trio
leaves. The model never deletes people because another age of them is
present.

### The crystal: a vigil that outlasts the battle

The ending doesn't show the crystal after the final battle. In this version
she is still there, unseen: the conventional reading. What the theory adds
is why she keeps sleeping once Demise is defeated (his remnant is sealed in
the sword, and her seal still holds it) and why she wakes when she does
(the wish ends that remnant at its original time). Keep the overlap with
the sword's own seal visible as a cost.

This is not the version 1 idea of a crystal immune to rewrites. She is not
protected from revision. She simply lives through H3, as Impa does.

### What the corrected present contains

The correction takes effect from Demise's defeat in the distant past, so
the entire present-day adventure belongs to the revised history. The
script fixes only part of H3's present:

- **Kept:** every protected crossing (Impa's arrival in 07, the desert
  escape in 10, Link's gate trips in 11/12 and 14/15, the departure in
  18, the return in 25), the wish result at its original time (W1), and
  everything earlier in calendar time than the correction, including
  the planted tree. Ghirahim must exist in the present until his crossing
  in 18. His arriving stage falls with Demise, and his native stage still
  leads to that crossing, as young Impa's does. He also needs no inherited
  motive. With Demise defeated in the distant past, Ghirahim's own era
  holds nothing to revive, so the past is his only option, exactly as in
  scene 18's H2 caption. The crystal vigil continues until W1, where the
  wish ends the remnant in the sword and Zelda wakes; her departure in 18
  follows from that.
- **Removed:** the Imprisoned and its present-day attacks.
- **Left open:** the ordinary events between those crossings, such as
  Zelda's fall, Ghirahim's pursuit, the trials, and the seedling and cure,
  which still rely on causes inherited from H0 and H1 (rule 2).

The presentation does not reconstruct H3's version of the adventure and
must not imply that it does. Scenes 24, W1, 25, and 26 show only what the
rules fix. List the open part among the limits in scene 27.

### The gates and Timeshift Stones: limits of the model

Use the desert Gate of Time for Zelda and Impa's escape, and the Temple of
Hylia / Sealed Temple gate for Link's visits. The two locations are not
interchangeable. “Forgotten Temple” is not the location name to use here.

Impa's younger self uses the temple gate in this reconstruction; the game
does not show this proposed meeting and departure. Their route is an
assumption, not a new canon fact.

The final gate closes/disappears after the party returns. Do not insist
that it is only dormant, and do not assert that its destruction erases
every earlier use. The exact relationship between its past and present
endpoints is not settled by this theory.

Timeshift Stones expose local earlier states rather than transporting
the whole party through a gate. The draft interprets Lanayru's lasting cure
as a historical revision, without claiming that every temporary stone
activation permanently rewrites the world. A complete theory still needs
to explain that boundary. Do not date the gorge's earlier state to the
same hour or necessarily the same era as Hylia's temple.
For the cure sequence, the model treats Link's interaction across the
Timeshift boundary as a temporal intervention whose experienced sequence
is retained. This application of traveler continuity is also an assumption;
it does not make every resident who ever sees a Timeshift Stone immune.

## Status

This is a presentation-ready narrative of the four-rule model, not a proof
of a uniquely consistent interpretation of the game. The earlier attempt is
this same journey as history first recorded it, ending in a resealing;
Link's changes revise it until the history's ending is the one its own
travelers reach. The sword needs no physical reset. Protected crossings connect the returning trio to the
revised personal histories, the sleeping Zelda's line runs unbroken from
the desert escape to her departure in 18, and the fulfilled wish keeps its
result at its original time, ending whatever remains of Demise then.

The earlier version's ending, and why it only resealed Demise, remain
hypothetical backstory; so does whether it replaced an even earlier one.
Traveler continuity and wish protection are stipulated rules. That the
wish reaches the remnant in the sword, and that Zelda's seal holds it until
then, are the theory's readings; for thousands of years her seal overlaps
with the sword's. The seedling and Lanayru's cure still rely on causes
inherited from H0 and H1, and the corrected present between the preserved
crossings is deliberately left open. The script should explain these
assumptions clearly without presenting them as new canonical scenes or a
demonstrated universal theory.
