# Field notes of Gorko, surface explorer — the sleeper continues

*The corrected history (H3) under the alternative in
`alternative-sleeper-continues.md`, where the crystal-bound Zelda is not
erased but sleeps until the statue lands. Seen from the ground: Gorko
knows only what he sees, what he is told, and the legends he has studied.
For the current, erasure version, see `gorko-chronicle.md`.*

---

**On what I knew before the storm**

For anyone who reads these notes later, here is what every Goron of the surface already knew before any of this began.

The Sealed Grounds are a quiet place. There is a great spiral pit there, and the old tales say something terrible was once sealed beneath it. It was destroyed long ago, in the age of the goddess, by a hero no one can name. Nothing stirs in that pit now but wind.

Beside the pit stands the Sealed Temple. An old woman of the Sheikah lives there, and as far as I know she has always lived there. My grandfather's grandfather spoke of her. She guards a sword set in a stone pedestal, and a great tree grows right inside the temple, its roots through the floor and its crown up to the roof. Nothing that size grows in a lifetime.

She guards something else too. Behind the temple's inner door, a girl sleeps inside a block of golden crystal. The old woman let me see her once, when I was young and asked too many questions. The girl hasn't aged a day in all the generations my family has been coming here. I asked who she was. The old woman said, "Someone worth waiting for." I asked when she would wake. "When it's time."

I've never understood what the sleeper guards against. The darkness in the pit was destroyed ages ago. When I said so, the old woman put her hand on the pedestal sword. "Destroyed is not the same as gone," she said. "What is left of him rests in this blade. The blade holds him, and so does she."

The Thunder Dragon lives up in the storm clouds over Lanayru, as he always has.

And some days before the storm, I saw a light inside the temple, and a young Sheikah woman came out. She had the same eye on her clothes and the same bearing as the old woman. She set off toward Faron Woods without a word. The old woman only smiled.

**The storm**

Today a black whirlwind tore open the clouds above Faron. It didn't drift, it *hunted*. Something fell out of it: a girl, a speck of pink, dropping through the sky into the forest. The whirlwind closed up and vanished as if it had finished a job.

I searched until dark and found nothing.

**The boy in green**

A boy came down from the sky on a red bird, dressed all in green, with a blue blade on his back that spoke to him. He asked everyone about a girl named Zelda.

He went to the old woman first, and she took him behind the inner door. I was outside, but I heard him cry out. When he came back his face was white. Later he told me the girl in the crystal looks *exactly* like Zelda. I told him that was impossible, since the sleeper has been there since before my grandfather's grandfather. He didn't argue. He just looked at the door.

**The white-haired man**

There is a man in the woods with white hair, dressed like a nobleman going to a dance, who fights like a demon. He calls himself a Demon Lord. He is after the girl, and I think the storm was his doing too.

The forest dwellers say the girl stopped at the spring with the young Sheikah woman and left before the boy arrived. She wore white now, not pink.

White, like the girl in the crystal.

**The desert**

I was in Lanayru when it happened.

The girl and the Sheikah woman ran to the old Temple of Time with the Demon Lord close behind. The boy held him off, and behind them a gate of light opened. The two women stepped into it, and the gate *shattered*. They destroyed it themselves on the way through.

That night I couldn't sleep. A girl in white walks into a gate that leads to another age. A girl in white has slept in a crystal for that age and every one since. I'm an explorer, not a seer, but I can add two and two.

**The flames, and a sword I recognized**

The boy visited the three sacred flames, and after the last one his blade had a new shape: the shape of the old woman's pedestal sword. Same guard, same blade. His is new and shining, and hers is old and still.

I'm starting to think everything in that temple is waiting for something it has already seen.

**The temple gate**

With the new sword the boy woke a gate inside the Sealed Temple and stepped through. He came back soon after, older around the eyes. When I asked where he'd been, he said he'd talked with Zelda, and that she had asked him to find something called the Triforce.

"Then she'll wake," he said. He didn't sound like he was guessing.

He went through the gate more than once. Once he came back with a seedling, went in, and returned with empty hands. The old woman looked up at her great tree and smiled.

**The search**

After that, the boy spent a long time in the sky and in trials the old woman called "silent." This time I knew why. Every time he passed through the temple he stopped at the inner door before going on.

He went to the gorge where the Thunder Dragon once lived, though the dragon has always lived in the clouds as far as I know. What he wanted there I couldn't say.

**The goddess comes down**

Today the sky fell.

The statue of the goddess came down through the clouds and landed in the spiral pit, with a golden light of three triangles blazing above it. The ground shook all the way to Eldin. It landed on an empty pit. Whatever she was meant to crush was destroyed ages ago.

I saw no one call her down. But I didn't wonder for long. The golden light reached across to the temple, and a light answered from inside it. I ran.

**The sleeper wakes**

The crystal was open.

Something dark was rising out of the pedestal sword, thin as smoke from a snuffed wick, and the golden light took it apart until there was nothing left. The old blade looked lighter afterward, the way a man looks when he finally sets down a load he's carried for too long. The girl stood in the middle of the temple, blinking as if she'd only shut her eyes a moment ago. She was the girl from the desert, the girl from the storm. Zelda. She had walked into the past and slept her way back to us, all those ages, right under my nose.

The old woman knelt to her. I've never seen her kneel to anyone.

**Taken again**

It lasted only a moment.

The Demon Lord came out of nowhere and seized her. He laughed about the age when his master could still be raised, and dragged her straight through the temple gate. The boy went after them, and so did a tall Skyloft lad with hair like a breaking wave. All four were gone.

The old woman didn't move. She had guarded that sleep for longer than anyone can count, and when it ended she stood and watched the girl carried off, as if she'd seen it a thousand times and knew how it ended.

**The return**

The gate stayed lit, and I stayed too.

Three people came back out: the boy, the girl, and the tall lad. The boy's hands were empty. The girl looked at the open crystal, the pedestal, and the old woman, and she smiled like someone who had finally reached the end of a very long road.

The old woman called her by name and showed her a bracelet on her wrist. The girl knew it at once. They spoke quietly, and I didn't come closer.

Then the old woman began to fade, like morning fog. She was smiling. Then she was gone, and the bracelet stayed where she had stood.

The sword is still in its pedestal. The crystal is empty. The gate has gone dark.

**What I can't answer**

Most of the puzzle has come together for me. The girl in the crystal and the girl in the storm were one girl, at two ends of her life. She left through the desert and came back through the crystal. I watched her whole road. I just read it out of order.

I never saw who asked the goddess to come down, and to my eyes she came down on an empty pit. But I watched where her light went. It went for the old sword, for whatever was left of the demon king in it, and it took that away. When I asked the old woman who had made the wish, she said it had been made "long ago, and only just now, both." I've stopped expecting her answers to make sense the first time.

I know now what the girl was holding, and why she woke. She held the seal on what was left of him, alongside the blade, and the day nothing was left of him, there was nothing left to hold. I still don't know why two locks were needed where one would seem to do.

And I don't know how the old woman bore it. She watched over that sleep knowing exactly what was waiting for the girl the moment she woke, and she said nothing. I think she knew that if she stopped that one cruel moment, she'd lose everything that came after it.

When the three of them talk, they speak of a great beast in the pit. I've lived beside that pit all my life and never saw it. But the girl doesn't speak of the beast. She slept through all of it, in their world and in mine.

*— Gorko*
